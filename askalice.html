Research, then verify.

Four steps between "I found a source" and "this article is ready to publish". You write the words — Alice verifies them.

1

Research

Ask 15+ free indexes and two resolvers. Each source says how it answered, and a blocked one links to where you connect it.

2

Check

A claim comes back with its verdict, the quote it rests on, the page and character offsets — and what would prove it wrong.

3

Cite

A citation that renders, in wikitext or APA/MLA/Chicago. Never circular: Wikipedia is a pointer, and a pointer is never a reference.

4

Audit & build

Check your draft sentence by sentence — exact wording for quotes, "verify meaning" for your own words — then build the finished edit with its references.

Built for Wikipedia.

The outcome is an article that survives review — not one that gets reverted for a dodgy citation or an unsourced sentence.

Non-circular citations

A claim found on Wikipedia descends to the source it cites. Alice refuses to cite the encyclopedia itself as evidence.

Talk-page requests

A ready-made edit request with the source and the quote — the sanctioned route for conflicted or paid editors.

References in one place

Every source you cite gathers into one copyable block for the References section, deduped and non-circular.
