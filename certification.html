Certification

VDS Certified — audited, version-scoped, revocable.

The standard is open. Certification is the audited product: a signed conformance report, a public registry entry, and a mark that carries its own scope — so anyone can check it rather than trust it.

The audit, in five steps

1 · Apply

Send the model

A candidate manifest, the evidence bundle, and read access to the model + data.

2 · Run

Six checks

The conformance suite recomputes the deficit, gates, mutations, reviewer independence and evidence.

3 · Review

Independent check

A sample of gates and mutation tests is re-run; the evidence bundle is confirmed.

4 · Decide

Pass or gaps

Pass → certificate. Fail → a written list of the gaps.

5 · Publish

The registry

Version, level, deficit, guards proven and the report hash, in public.

Mark

Carries its scope

product v{version} · audited {date} · L3 · m={m} · report hash — no evergreen badge.

This is the badge

A certified site wears it in its footer. It states its own scope — product, version, audit date, guards proven, gates structural — so a reader can check it rather than trust it.

Version-scoped and revocable: change the version and the badge lapses. Every line on it is a fact the audit produced, not a claim the site makes about itself.

What the mark means

  • It is version-scoped and time-scoped.
  • It certifies the verification discipline — never the model's truth.
  • It is awarded at L3 only: L2 proven guards + a computed deficit + a certificate + the evidence bundle.
  • It is a licence, not a purchase — it lapses when the version changes.

Revocation (no grace period)

  • A green-when-nothing-ran regression.
  • A claimed guard that does not go red on its seeded fault.
  • The reviewer running on the same model as the coder.
  • An unproven guard added silently.

Revocations are public in the same registry, with the reason and the date.
